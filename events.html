🔬 Division A Events

2026–2027 Season · Grades 3–5 · 22 events across 3rd Grade and Upper Division

Division A events are organized into two tiers for students in grades 3–5. 3rd Grade events are designed specifically for 3rd graders; Upper Division events are open to all grades 3–5. Click any event name to open the full rules PDF.

3rd Grade

3rd Grade Events

5 events · designed for 3rd grade students

🎈

Balloon Racer

3rd Grade · Engineering · 45 min · 2 students

Teams design and build an air-powered balloon device that races along a fishing line track, aiming for the shortest travel time. A simultaneous written assessment covers Newton's Laws of Motion and air pressure — including estimating their racer's travel time before launch.

What to bring: ANSI Z87+ safety glasses REQUIRED  · writing utensils · scissors

🎨

Picture This

3rd Grade · Inquiry · 30 min · 3 students

One teammate silently draws scientific terms on a whiteboard while the other two guess; no speaking allowed except to pass. Terms span life, earth, space, and physical science. Teammates rotate roles, trying to correctly identify as many terms as possible in 5-minute rounds.

What to bring: 2 black dry-erase markers  (no notes of any kind allowed)

🌿

Biomes

3rd Grade · Life Science · 30 min · 2 students

Written test or stations covering ecology fundamentals (food chains, food webs, species interactions), major terrestrial and aquatic biomes (tundra, rainforest, desert, coral reef, etc.), biodiversity, human impacts on ecosystems, and data analysis using biome maps and ecological diagrams.

What to bring: pen/pencil · optional 4-function calculator  (no notes of any kind allowed)
Rules PDF ↗
🌉

Truss Bridge

3rd Grade · Engineering · 45 min · 2 students

Teams build a truss bridge from paper straws and tape spanning a 30 cm gap (≥10 cm wide and ≥10 cm tall at midspan). Tested with a 300g mass held for 30 seconds; bonus for 500g. A simultaneous written assessment covers bridge types, structural forces (tension, compression, shear, torsion), and load distribution.

What to bring: ANSI Z87+ safety glasses REQUIRED  · pencil/pen/marker · ruler or straightedge · scissors (one per member)

🫀

Human Body

3rd Grade · Life Science · 30 min · 2 students

Written test or stations on the structure, function, and organization of 9 major body systems: Skeletal, Muscular, Digestive, Respiratory, Circulatory, Excretory, Nervous, Sensory, and Endocrine. May include labeled diagrams, medical images, and scenario-based problems.

What to bring: pen/pencil · optional 4-function calculator  (no notes of any kind allowed)

Upper Division Events

17 events · Grades 3–5

🚀

Astronaut Lander

Upper Division · Engineering · 45 min · 2 students

Teams go through a mock purchasing process (≤$200 budget) to choose materials, then build a landing device to protect a bouncy ball "astronaut" dropped from a designated height. Scored on drop hang time (longer = better), budget efficiency, and a written assessment on reentry physics.

What to bring: ANSI Z87+ safety glasses REQUIRED  · completed Shopping List MANDATORY (30% penalty without it)  · writing utensils · scissors · optional 4-function calculator
Rules PDF ↗
⭐

Astronomy

Upper Division · Earth & Space Science · 30 min · 2 students

Written test or stations on star formation (nebulae → protostar → main sequence), stellar classification using H-R diagrams and spectral classes, evolutionary pathways (low-mass: red giant → white dwarf; high-mass: supernova → neutron star or black hole), and the Sun's layers and energy production. Key objects include Proxima Centauri, Sirius, Betelgeuse, Orion Nebula, and the Crab Nebula.

What to bring: pen/pencil · optional 4-function calculator  (no notes of any kind allowed)
Rules PDF ↗
🪂

Bungee Drop

Upper Division · Engineering · 45 min · 2 students

Teams build a rubber band bungee cord system to drop a 100g water bottle weight from 200–500 cm, aiming to stop as close to the "artificial horizon" (zero line) as possible without passing it (penalty ×3 if it does). Two drops; best counts. Simultaneous written test on conservation of energy, elastic potential energy, gravitational potential energy, and kinetic energy.

What to bring: ANSI Z87+ safety glasses REQUIRED  · writing utensils · optional ruler/meter stick · optional scissors
Rules PDF ↗
🔐

Codebusters

Upper Division · Inquiry · 30 min · 3 students

Teams decrypt encrypted messages using 7 cipher types: Aristocrats (with hint), Atbash, Caesar/Shift, PigPen/Masonic, Polybius Square, Running Man, and Vigenère (key provided). A bonus Simple Cryptarithm may also appear. No code exceeds 9th-grade readability. Only the VASO-provided Codebusters Resources Sheet may be used — no other notes.

What to bring: pen/pencil · optional 4-function calculator · VASO Div A Codebusters Resources Sheet  (no other notes allowed)

Energy

Upper Division · Physical Science · 30 min · 2 students

Written test or stations on energy forms (kinetic, potential, thermal, chemical, electrical, nuclear), the law of conservation of energy, energy transfer (conduction, convection, radiation), renewable vs. nonrenewable sources, and real-world power facility case studies worldwide covering solar, wind, hydroelectric, geothermal, biomass, coal, oil, natural gas, and nuclear plants.

What to bring: pen/pencil · optional 4-function calculator  (no notes of any kind allowed)

Genetics

Upper Division · Life Science · 30 min · 2 students

Written test or stations on genetics vocabulary (DNA, chromosomes, genes, alleles, genotype/phenotype), DNA structure and base pairing, Punnett squares and probability for monohybrid crosses, common inherited human traits (earlobe attachment, tongue rolling, colorblindness, ACHOO syndrome), and inheritance patterns beyond simple dominance: codominance, incomplete dominance, and X-linked traits.

What to bring: pen/pencil · optional 4-function calculator  (no notes of any kind allowed)

Glider

Upper Division · Engineering · 45 min · 2 students

Teams build a glider of any design from a family-size cereal box plus provided tape and paperclips (≥2 paperclips required). Two official launches; longest flight time wins. A simultaneous written test covers Lift, Drag, Weight, Thrust, Bernoulli's Principle, center of gravity, L/D Ratio, Aspect Ratio, Dihedral, and Reflex. Teams also estimate their flight time as part of scoring.

What to bring: ANSI Z87+ safety glasses REQUIRED  · 1 family-size cereal box (~18–20 oz, approx 38×20×7.6 cm) REQUIRED  · scissors · ruler/straightedge · writing utensils

Hovercraft

Upper Division · Engineering · 45 min · 2 students

Teams build a balloon-powered CD hovercraft from provided materials (CD, balloon, mounting putty, pop-top, tape, cardstock) in 15 minutes, then choose a starting position on a 122 cm ramp and release it to glide as close to the finish line (0.5–1.5 m from ramp end) as possible without crossing it. Two runs; better run counts. Simultaneous written test on hovercraft design, potential and kinetic energy, and friction.

What to bring: ANSI Z87+ safety glasses REQUIRED  · pencils · scissors · optional straight edge · optional log of previous test runs (no other notes)

Hydraulics

Upper Division · Engineering · 45 min · 2 students

Teams build a freestanding hydraulic device using two syringes connected by vinyl tubing to lift a 100g weight off the floor as high as possible and hold it for up to 30 seconds. A simultaneous written test covers hydraulics principles including Pascal's Law, pressure, force transmission, and fluid mechanics.

What to bring: ANSI Z87+ safety glasses REQUIRED  · pencils · scissors · optional straight edge/ruler

Optics

Upper Division · Physical Science · 30 min · 2 students

Written test on light properties (reflection, refraction, image formation, color, electromagnetic spectrum, optical devices) plus a 2-minute hands-on laser challenge: position up to 3 movable mirrors (plus 1 fixed mirror) to redirect a laser beam to a target dot. Scored on written accuracy, laser accuracy, and number of attempts used.

What to bring: pen/pencil · optional 4-function calculator · optional protractor & ruler · optional notecard (to trace laser path)  (no safety glasses required for this event)

Periodic Table

Written test and possible hands-on stations covering periodic table organization (periods, groups, metals/nonmetals/metalloids), element families (alkali metals, alkaline earth metals, halogens, noble gases, transition metals), atomic structure (protons, neutrons, electrons, atomic number/mass), periodic trends (atomic radius, electronegativity, ionization energy, reactivity), and valence electrons and chemical behavior.

What to bring: pen/pencil · optional 4-function calculator  (no notes of any kind allowed)

Teams use provided Virginia road maps and USGS 7.5-minute topographic maps to answer questions on map symbols/legends, compass bearings, coordinates, political boundaries, distance calculations using map scale, contour line interpretation (elevation, landforms, percent slope), and road navigation (shortest routes, driving directions).

Rubber Band Helicopter

Upper Division · Engineering · 45 min · 2 students

Teams build a rubber band helicopter from provided materials (propeller, craft stick, paperclip, rubber bands, cardstock, tape, plasticine). Two launches; average flight time is the score. A written test covers rotary flight principles. Teams must bring a pre-competition testing chart with ≥6 test results using different rubber band sizes and weights — used as a tiebreaker.

What to bring: ANSI Z87+ safety glasses REQUIRED (during test phase)  · pencils · ruler/straight edge · scissors · testing chart with ≥6 results MANDATORY

Sail Car

Upper Division · Engineering · 45 min · 2 students

Teams build a sail-powered model car from provided materials (wheels, straws, skewers, craft sticks, tissue paper, cardstock, cardboard, tape, plasticine) in 30 minutes, then race it on a fan-powered 3-meter track. Scored on travel time plus deviation from the centerline — lower is better. One official scored run. Simultaneous written test on wind, angles, and aerodynamics. Requesting extra materials places the team in Tier 2 (lower scoring tier).

What to bring: ANSI Z87+ safety glasses REQUIRED  · writing utensils · ruler or straight edge · scissors  (no notes of any kind allowed)

Tower Building

Upper Division · Engineering · 45 min · 2 students

Teams build a freestanding tower from 30 paper drinking straws and 1 meter of masking tape in 25 minutes. Scored on tower height (tallest = best), maximum load held (50g, 100g, then 300g, each for 30 seconds), and a written test on tower types, structural forces (tension, compression, shear, torsion), stability, center of gravity, and failure modes.

What to bring: ANSI Z87+ safety glasses REQUIRED  · pencil/pen/marker · optional ruler/straightedge · optional scissors · optional 4-function calculator

Anatomy

Upper Division · Life Science · 30 min · 2 students

Written test or stations focused entirely on the human digestive system: organ anatomy and functions, the full pathway of food from ingestion to elimination, mechanical vs. chemical digestion, roles of saliva/stomach acid/bile/enzymes, how carbohydrates/proteins/fats are broken down and absorbed, major nutrient groups, balanced diet, and common digestive disorders (GERD, lactose intolerance, peptic ulcers, appendicitis).

Super Sleuths

Upper Division · Inquiry · 30 min · 2 students

Teams receive a crime scenario, evidence samples, and a suspect list, then perform lab tests (solubility, magnetism, pH, color, texture) on powders, liquids, and metals; analyze polymers, hair types, fiber types, and recyclable plastics; examine fingerprint patterns (whorls/loops/arches), shoe/tire prints, and DNA chromatograms; then write a formal crime scene analysis naming the most likely suspect with scientific evidence and reasoning.

What to bring: ANSI Z87+ indirect vent goggles (Category C) REQUIRED  · pen/pencil  (no notes of any kind allowed; regular safety glasses are not sufficient for this event)
